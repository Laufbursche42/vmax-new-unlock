## Raising the top speed removes the limiter and voids the road approval (ABE)

The main function raises the top speed. This **removes the manufacturer's limiter**.

> **Raising the top speed voids the ABE.** A vehicle changed this way no longer matches the state in which it was type-approved; its general operating permit (ABE) **becomes void**. From that moment it is **not** an approved vehicle and use in public traffic is **not allowed**.

## Public-road use is then very likely a criminal offence

Once the ABE is void, riding in public traffic is no longer a mere administrative offence.

> **Riding it in public traffic very likely constitutes criminal offences.** In particular:
>
> - **Driving without the required licence:** the vehicle is no longer covered by the class under which it was approved.
> - **Breach of the compulsory insurance act:** compulsory insurance cover lapses together with the approval, so operating it without insurance is a punishable offence.
>
> Both are punishable. Possible consequences include fines, penalty points, loss of licence and imprisonment; you can also be held personally liable for any damage you cause.

## Serious risk of injury and crashes

> **A faster scooter is a more dangerous scooter.** Brakes, tyres, frame and lights are designed for the approved speed. Beyond it, the risk of losing control, fading brakes, tyre failure, a serious crash and severe or fatal injury rises sharply, for you and for others.
